# Ozone safety and the BreatheIQ interlock

Sources: US EPA National Ambient Air Quality Standard for ozone (2015); US EPA, "Ozone Generators
that are Sold as Air Cleaners"; California Air Resources Board (CARB) air-cleaner regulation;
OSHA and NIOSH occupational limits.

## Reference limits

| Body | Limit | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| US EPA | 0.070 ppm, 8-hour average | Ambient air standard for the general public |
| CARB | 0.050 ppm | Maximum ozone emission concentration allowed for indoor air cleaners |
| OSHA | 0.10 ppm, 8-hour time-weighted average | Workplace permissible exposure limit |
| NIOSH | 0.10 ppm ceiling | Recommended workplace limit, never to be exceeded |

The EPA advises that ozone generators should not be used in occupied spaces: at concentrations
that do not exceed public health standards ozone has little effect on indoor pollutants, and at
higher concentrations it irritates the airways. Children, older adults, people with asthma or
other lung disease and people active outdoors are the most sensitive to ozone.

## The interlock

The BreatheIQ coach carries two ozone generators as purifiers. The model may request them, but the
safety interlock decides:

1. Ozone may run only when the cabin is unoccupied.
2. The estimated 8-hour ozone average must be below 0.07 ppm and the instantaneous estimate below 0.10 ppm.
3. If either condition fails the request is BLOCKED, the exhaust fan and filtration run instead,
   and the decision is written to the interlock log with its reason.

A reading such as 0.05 ppm is under the EPA 8-hour standard of 0.07 ppm, but the interlock does not
rely on that margin for anyone on board: no ozone is generated while any passenger, including a
pregnant passenger, is in the coach. BreatheIQ gives air-quality guidance only; it does not give
medical advice. A passenger with a health concern should speak to train staff or a doctor.
